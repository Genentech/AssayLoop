What biology does each method go after?

Two methods can recover the same number of hits from completely different parts of the cell. Effective Pathways counts how much distinct Reactome biology a method's picks span — at the scale of a single 100-gene batch, a whole screen, and the full test set. The vocabulary is Reactome's 186 level-2 groups (Innate Immune System, Cell Cycle Mitotic, Signaling by Receptor Tyrosine Kinases, …), so the numbers read as counts of named biological programs. For reference, a uniform draw from the acquisition universe scores 21.8 / 56.4 / 82.1 at the three scopes.

Effective pathways by scope

How the metric works

  • Level-2 groups, not leaf sets. The GMT's 2012 leaf pathway sets are too fine to count with: over 10,480 annotated genes, thirty genes drawn one pathway each essentially never collide, so EP-B was pinned just under its own reference count for every method (24.5–27.6 out of 30) and measured "how close to M did you get" rather than how much biology the batch touched. Each leaf is therefore lifted to its Reactome level-2 group — the direct children of the 29 top-level roots, the same tier the sunbursts' outer ring draws. 185 are populated by this GMT, plus one bucket for the 36 leaves with no hierarchy entry, for 186 in all.
  • One group per gene. A gene in k groups contributes a single sampled group, not 1/k to each. Fractional weights invert the batch-scope ranking — they reward picking promiscuous, heavily-annotated genes.
  • Effective count, not raw count. The score is exp(H) for Shannon entropy H over that distribution, so thirty picks spread evenly over thirty groups scores thirty, and thirty picks that are twenty-nine in one group plus one stray scores close to one.
  • Rarefaction. Entropy grows with sample size, so every method is scored on the same number of annotated genes per unit — — per batch, — per screen, — across the dataset. Without this, a method that simply submits more valid genes would look more diverse.
  • Truncated batches are dropped. A batch that came up short is a run artifact, not a narrow batch. A full batch whose genes happen not to be in Reactome is kept — that is a real property of the method's picks.

What that looks like

The same picks, drawn rather than counted. Each sunburst is one method's proposed genes laid out over Reactome: the inner ring is the eight largest top-level categories plus everything else, the outer ring the second tier the metric above counts over. A colour means the same category in every panel. The number at the centre is the effective pathway count — the number of equally-weighted groups that would give the same spread.

Does breadth cost enrichment?

One last check, not in the paper. The obvious worry about optimising for hit rate is that a policy learns to mine one pathway. Plot the enrichment factor against pathway coverage and see whether that happened. Each point is a method; hover for its name.

A dash in place of a number is a refusal to score, not a zero: a method whose picks are mostly unannotated cannot supply enough genes to calculate the metric.
