Method

How the loop is built

AssayLoop is three pieces: a benchmark that turns historical CRISPR screens into a sequential decision problem, a transformer that learns an acquisition policy across all of them, and a handoff rule that lets an LLM open the screen before the policy takes over. This page walks the pipeline one panel at a time, then defines every metric the paper reports.

The metrics

Seven metrics, each measuring something a single hit count cannot. The definitions below are the paper's. Every one of them is a column in the results table →

Hit enrichment factor (EF)

Our main metric is the hit enrichment factor (EF), that measures the ratio between the number of hits found and the expected number of hits found by random selection. Writing NL for the number of acquired genes over the full trajectory present in the screen library, NḠ for the number of acquired genes that do not represent valid genes (i.e. hallucinated genes), and hrand = |H|/|L| for the random hit rate, the hit enrichment factor is

EF = |GT ∩ H| ⁄  [ (NL + NḠ) · hrand ]

This formula differs from the classical enrichment factor in the number of acquired genes used for the normalization. By normalizing with (NL + NḠ) we do not penalize acquisition of real genes that are not part of the screen library, but do penalize the acquisition of hallucinated genes. 1× is chance; 5.66× is the best method in the paper.

Normalized area under the cumulative-hits curve (nAUC)

We also report the normalized area under the cumulative-hits curve (nAUC), which captures the full acquisition trajectory rather than only the endpoint. We construct the curve by plotting the fraction of hits found against the fraction of the library effectively queried, with one point per acquisition batch. The area under this piecewise-linear curve is computed by trapezoidal integration and normalized by the AUC of a perfect oracle that ranks all hits before any non-hit, over the same effective budget. A value of 1.0 indicates the model acquires all hits in the screen first; random acquisition yields nAUC ≈ |H|/|L| — the hit rate — in expectation. Fraction of hits (FH)

We report the fraction of hits found over the whole trajectory, measuring the recall of the acquisition policy: the number of hits acquired by the model divided by the total number of hits in the screen. Only in-library genes can be hits, so out-of-library and hallucinated acquisitions do not contribute to the numerator. A value of 1.0 means all hits in the screen were found within the acquisition budget.

Shortfall (SF)

We report the shortfall as the fraction of acquired genes that fall outside the screen's gene library: the number of acquired genes not present in the screen library divided by the total number of acquisitions. This includes both real genes outside the library and hallucinated genes. It is the cost that EF alone hides — an LLM proposing genes that were never in the screen has spent real budget on them.

Percentage of essential genes (%ess)

Some genes are hits in a given screen just because these genes typically lead to cell death in general, also known as essential genes. While these genes are genuine hits, they do not always reflect the particularities of the biology studied in the screen. To reflect the number of found hits that are specific to the biology of interest, we also report the percentage of essential genes among the hits found — the number of acquired hits that are common essentials divided by the total number of acquired hits. We used the list of 1,827 common essential genes from the Cancer Dependency Map (DepMap).

Vendi score (VS)

For each batch, we compute gene embeddings using GenePT, construct the cosine-similarity Gram matrix of all acquired genes, and compute its Vendi score, which corresponds to the effective number of distinct genes in the batch — 1 means all gene embeddings are collinear, and |Gt| means all are orthogonal. We report the Vendi ratio, which is normalized by |Gt| and averaged across batches. A higher Vendi ratio means the genes acquired in the batch are more diverse.

Pathway overlap (PO)

As an embedding-free alternative, we measure the functional overlap of genes' pathways within each batch using pathway and GO-term annotations from MSigDB. For each pair of genes in the batch we compute the Jaccard similarity of their pathway sets, and average over pairs. To account for the baseline density of pathway annotations, we normalize by the mean pairwise Jaccard of a random draw of 512 genes from the annotated genome. Values above 1 indicate the acquired genes cluster in shared pathways more than expected by chance.

Effective pathways (EP-B / EP-S / EP-D)

We summarise how much distinct biology a method interrogates by the effective number of Reactome pathways its picks cover, EP = exp(H) — the Hill number of order 1 of the pathway distribution induced by a set of genes. Each annotated gene is assigned to one of its pathways uniformly at random and the estimate is averaged over draws; genes absent from the filtered GMT are dropped.

The random assignment matters. Splitting a gene's weight 1/k across all k of its pathways yields the same expected pathway distribution, but lets a bag of m genes span ∑ki categories rather than m, so a method favouring well-studied hub genes scores high on annotation depth alone — enough, in our data, to invert the batch-scope ranking. Because exp(H) is additionally sample-size sensitive, and methods differ in what fraction of their picks are annotated at all, each unit is subsampled to a fixed annotated-gene count before the entropy is taken.

We report three scopes: EP-B over a single acquisition batch (30 genes), EP-S over all picks within a screen (200), and EP-D over all picks pooled across the test set (6000), averaging over batches then screens for the first two. The reference counts are fixed constants rather than the minimum over the methods compared, so adding a baseline cannot change existing rows; a method that cannot supply its scope's count is reported as a dash. The scopes separate within-batch from across-screen diversity: a method may spread widely inside every batch yet revisit the same biology screen after screen, or concentrate locally while pursuing different programs across screens.
