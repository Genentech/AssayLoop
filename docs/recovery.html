Recovery curves

Ten rounds, one hundred genes each. The whole question is how fast the hits come in — and, for the methods that pick genes the screen never assayed, how much of the budget is spent outside the library at all. Pick any set of methods and watch both.

What to look for

  • The early rounds are where the handoff pays. Compare Gemini-3.1-pro against AssayFormer + BPMF + GRPO over the first two or three rounds, then against Gemini-3.1-Pro → AssayFormer over all ten. The LLM starts better and the learned policy finishes better; the handoff is an attempt to have both.
  • Switch the Y axis to "picks outside the screen's library." This is the cost that the enrichment factor alone hides: a method proposing real genes that this screen never assayed spends real budget on them and can never find a hit there. It is the part of the Shortfall column that comes from real genes, unrolled over rounds. The three finetuned Qwen rows sit at zero here by construction, not by virtue: their harness skipped a name it could not assay and read further down the model's list instead, so those picks cost nothing. What they cost shows up at the other end of the curve, which stops short of 1,000 genes when the list runs out first.
  • Switch to one screen at a time. The mean curve is smooth; the individual screens are not. Some screens are easy for everything and some separate the methods sharply.
