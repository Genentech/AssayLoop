Qualitative analysis

What the models learned

The enrichment factor says which method found more hits. It does not say what either of them was doing. These three analyses open up the picks themselves: how a handoff's composition changes at the moment the LLM gives way to the policy, which parts of the cell each LLM reaches for, and what learning that one gene is a hit does to AssayFormer's predictions for every other gene.

Composition of a handoff, round by round

Two screens with very different biology — an NF-κB / TNF signalling screen and an AAV transgene silencing screen — run three ways: AssayFormer alone, Gemini-3.1-Pro alone, and the handoff between them. The upper histogram is what each round proposed; the lower one is what hit. The handoff's early rounds look like the LLM's and its late rounds look like the policy's, which is the mechanism working as designed.

Which biology each LLM reaches for

Top-level Reactome composition of the genes each model requested, pooled over the 20 test screens, as a ratio against the share a uniform draw from the candidate universe would give. Above 1 is over-representation. The striking result is how similar the frontier models are to each other: they converge on much the same picture of what a CRISPR screen is about, regardless of the phenotype in the prompt. The diversity explorer quantifies the same effect as effective pathway counts.

What one hit tells AssayFormer about the rest

AssayFormer conditions on the genes already assayed and their outcomes, so it is possible to ask directly what one observation does to another prediction: fix a probe gene, observe it as a hit, and measure how every other gene's predicted hit probability moves, averaged over random background contexts. The result is a learned, context-conditional influence map. Some of the strongest pairs are in no standard interaction database.

The rows are twelve canonical cancer drivers used as probes; the columns are every gene the paper singles out as a response, which is nineteen functional-module genes plus ATM — ATM is on both axes, as a driver in its own right and as the response to PIK3CA.
